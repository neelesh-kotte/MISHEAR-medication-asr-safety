## What this establishes

Existing work establishes that medication words can be poorly represented by aggregate WER, synthetic TTS can support medication-vocabulary recognition, accent and domain shifts matter, phonetic information can aid entity correction, and FDA POCA screens name similarity. These are established ingredients, so none should be presented as the novelty by itself.

## Preliminary research gap

The reviewed sources do not appear to combine all of the following in one medication-specific, reproducible evaluation: (1) observed medication substitution labels from ASR outputs on original controlled synthetic recordings with measured SNR/RT60; (2) pair-level vulnerability prediction separated from recording-level error auditing; (3) ingredient-disjoint testing plus a separately held-out acoustic-condition family; (4) explanation/ablation across phonetic, lexical, contextual and measured acoustic features; (5) a validation-selected finite-sample false-alert threshold, with explicit testing of whether it holds under acoustic shift; and (6) cross-recognizer evaluation.

This is a **provisional gap statement**, not a priority or first-of-its-kind claim. The literature search must expand across medical ASR, medication-name error correction, clinical dictation post-processing, selective prediction/conformal risk control, phonetic drug-name similarity, synthetic speech evaluation, and 2025–2026 work. Compare closest methods and preprints/version histories; revise the contribution if equivalent work is found.

## Difference from POCA and correction tools

- **POCA** compares drug-name strings for orthographic and phonetic similarity in FDA’s proprietary-name review process. It does not take an audio recording or ASR hypothesis as input, and its score is not the probability that a particular recognizer will make a substitution. MISHEAR uses transparent published baselines and may report POCA only for reproducibly obtained scores; it will not infer POCA scores from its own metrics.
- **ASR entity-correction tools** generally attempt to replace or repair a recognizer’s output using entity catalogs, phonetic features, or language models. MISHEAR’s principal contribution is framed as prediction and evaluation, including a separate audit decision that can flag or abstain without automatically changing the medication token. Suggested alternatives are evaluated after the auditor predicts.
- **Medication ASR adaptation work** such as SynthASR modifies the recognizer to improve recognition. MISHEAR instead evaluates frozen recognizers and asks whether error vulnerability can be predicted across ingredients, acoustic conditions, and recognizer architectures. This does not imply that adaptation is unhelpful; it is a different question.
